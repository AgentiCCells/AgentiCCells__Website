What works. The home hero is calm and professional: one sentence, two buttons, a picture of a cell, a five-link nav (crop `home-1280-top.png`). /website-cell/ is the best page: four fact cards and a "Four ways to start" side panel, with the price marked as a proposal and "Nothing has been sold yet". Honest limits are everywhere ("What this page does not claim"), which a careful buyer will like.

What a buyer would stumble on:
1. **The offer is below the hero on the home page.** At 1280 px the first screen has the sentence, two buttons and the diagram, but no price, no "first proposal" and no status; the "£500" is in the paragraph under the fold (crop `home-1280-top.png`). A buyer cannot tell in five seconds what they would buy or what it costs. W-RV3 (same point, 16:12) is still open.
2. **The home page is one long column of text with no example and no way into the hub.** From "Start here" to "What this page does not claim" it is eleven text sections, about 4,050 px at 1280 px, and no section shows a real result. The hub now holds screenshots of six sites we made (three of them rich and good looking: RiskMandate at Web Summit, the orange grower, Gabriel DaCruz). The home page does not mention the hub. Under the split Dinis described, "Links to hub entries as examples" is this site's job. New W-RV9 and D-RV13.
3. **/cells/ now opens with the hub, not with what is sold.** Its first section, "Examples live on the hub", is two sentences that say what the page does *not* list; the four sizes start below it (crops `cells-1280-top.png`, `cells-390-top.png`). A buyer on /cells/ wants the four sizes first. The page is still 9,225 px on a phone, and W-RV4 (merge the repeated size blocks) is still open. New W-RV10.
4. **/our-team-as-a-cell/ is a proof page, not a sales page, and is the weakest on a phone** (19 small links, seven-item contents list). It is honest and sourced, and it ends with "How a customer gets the same" in the sixth of seven sections: a buyer reading in order meets the build log before the offer. It would sell better with that section first (one short paragraph and the link to /website-cell/ under the opening quote). New W-RV11. D-RV10, D-RV11 and W-RV8 (same page) stay open.
5. **Space.** At 1280 px the text column is 660 px and the right third is blank on the home page and /cells/, and the "Four properties" grid still leaves a column mismatch (D-RV11, D-RV12 open). I did not find a new problem here, only the same ones.

## The hub as its own site (hub.agenticcells.site v0.1.7)
Scores: first impression 4, what it is 5, polish 3, consistency with the other sites 4, mobile 3, trust 3.
Good: "Find a cell", search and three filters, a "demo or live" tag on every card, a stated "This hub lists; it does not sell", a link to agenticcells.ai, a per-cell page that says "Demo. We made this to show what a cell can do" and "Version v0.1.131, seen 2026-11-16. Source: the registry, not a live reading", screenshots with the time they were taken and the version then live. This is honest and reads as a catalogue (crop `hub-1280-top.png`). The three site cards with screenshots (RiskMandate, the orange grower, Gabriel) are the best on the page.

Problems:
1. **Eleven of the 17 cards are placeholders, and nine of those are not things that exist yet.** Six "Specialist: ..." cards, the email cell, two "Factory pipeline test" cards and the template show a striped box with the kind's name and "No live address, no version" (hub full page; crop `hub-specialist-390.png`). A visitor to a catalogue sees more empty boxes than cells. Two of the 11 (site factory, template) are real parts but have nothing to show. The page for "Specialist: code" says "Screenshots: None" and "Demo. We made this to show what a cell can do", but the registry's own text calls these proposals from a newsroom article, not things made. Calling an unmade proposal a "Demo" overstates; "Proposed" or "Planned" would be true. The two factory tests are our own test runs, not cells a buyer could use.
2. **Order and default view bury the good ones.** The default filter is "Demo and live" with no sort; the three rich cards sit in row 2 behind the two meta-sites (the company site and the hub itself, whose card is a screenshot of the hub). Put the cells with screenshots first, then the rest; or default the filter to "With an address".
3. **Card thumbnails are unreadable.** The first three show a whole page shrunk into 360 px, so the text is a grey smear (crop `hub-1280-top.png`); the dark ones work because the hero is the image. Crop the thumbnail to the top of the page at 1366 px so the headline is legible.
4. **On a phone the filters push the first card below the fold.** The heading, a seven-line paragraph and three filters plus a search box take the whole first screen at 390 px (crop not kept); a Hugging Face or GitHub list shows results first. Shorten the paragraph, put the filters in a "Filter" disclosure, show the card.
5. **Version stamps are stale in the page of its own first card.** The hub says the company site is v0.1.131; it is v0.1.139. It is dated and labelled "not a live reading", which is honest, but the first card a buyer sees looks behind. A scheduled rebuild or a live read of /version.json at build time would fix it (open since the last review).
6. Small tap targets: 17 links under 32 px high at 390 px, mainly the tags and card titles.
