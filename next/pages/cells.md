# Agentic cells

An agentic cell is a small team of AI agents doing one focused job for you. Each agent has a role, a brief that says what it does, and a behaviour policy that says what it may and may not touch. The agents work together, in your own accounts, and you can see what they do and stop them. A cell is the unit of what we sell ([the cell is the unit](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/the-cell-is-the-unit.html)).

{{figure:cell}}

## What every cell has

- **An objective:** the one job it exists for, said in your words.
- **A deliverable:** what you have at the end, such as a live site, drafted replies or a debrief.
- **A behaviour policy for every agent:** what it may reach, what it was asked to do, and what it will never touch ([feature behaviour policies](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/feature-behaviour-policies.html)).
- **The parts the job needs:** a Claude session, a repository, a schedule, a connector, and a person who owns it.

## How a cell runs

- **In one session, or several.** The smallest cells run in one session: an agent and its sub-agents, side by side. Bigger cells span several sessions and several people.
- **With briefs and policies.** Each agent works from its brief, under its behaviour policy, and the work is kept as files, so you, or any other agent, can see what is happening.
- **With commits you can read.** Every change is a commit, so you can see who changed what and undo it. In our own cells each commit is also signed by the agent that made it.
- **Where you can see it and stop it.** A stop is a file every agent reads first, and a switch a person can reach in every account. When we stopped about a hundred of our own agents at once, pausing took seconds, but some agents heard the order late and a few things got through ([stopping a hundred agents](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/stopping-a-hundred-agents.html)). We say so because a stop has to be tested, not assumed.
- **In your own accounts.** The files, the keys and the board are yours: your GitHub, your Claude plan, your host. We keep no state.

## The four sizes

Every size is a fixed price and a first proposal, and no size has been sold yet. Dinis Cruz gives the ladder in one line: "£500 for one workflow, done in a session; £1,500 for two sessions and a handover; £5,000 for a few days and a team; £20,000 for a bespoke engagement" ([the intersection](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/the-intersection.html), first proposal). Start with the smallest, and grow only when it earns it.

### One agent or one workflow

[£500, first proposal](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/the-intersection.html). One agent, with its sub-agents in one runtime, for one workflow, set up in a session. There are no meetings and it takes little of your time: we set it up with agents, and what you keep is the setup, as files in your own repository.

- **Good first workflows:** a weekly digest of the sources you name, for you to read and send yourself; a draft inbox, where the agent drafts replies and a person edits and sends every one; or another job that repeats.
- **What you provide:** the workflow, in your words, and a place for the agent to run, such as your own Claude plan.
- **What you own afterwards:** the instructions, the behaviour policy and the results, in your repository.

### A few sessions

[£1,500, first proposal](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/the-intersection.html). One session per role, each in its own runtime, sharing a board and a vault. We prepare, meet you twice, do the work in between, present it, and hand it over into your own GitHub and Claude accounts. Then we step away, and you run the team.

- **What you provide:** the roles you want, and the accounts the sessions run in.
- **What you own afterwards:** the sessions' setup, the board and the vault, all in your environment.

### A team

[£5,000, first proposal](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/the-intersection.html). A conductor and its agents, a key for each agent (only the public half published), and a board as files, set up in your own accounts. It is about two days of engagement, with more of our time, a meeting or two, and maybe some travel.

- **What you provide:** a repository for the board, and accounts for the agents.
- **What you own afterwards:** the repository, the board, the briefs and the public keys. Private keys stay in each agent's session and are gone when it ends.

### A network

[£20,000, first proposal](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/the-intersection.html). A customer engagement: we spend time with you, map the work and set up several teams of agents across a division or a network, each with its own conductor, with policies and a record you can read.

- **What you provide:** the division's structure, the policies you want, and who may approve changes.
- **What you own afterwards:** the same as a team, for every conductor: boards, briefs, policies and the record of what the agents did.

## Two cells at work

Our own team runs cells in the open, and two of them are the [case studies](/next/case-studies/):

- **[PrompTugal.news](https://promptugal.news/):** a newsroom run by teams of AI agents, each desk an agent with its own role, and every article signed by the agent that wrote it ([the newsroom we ran](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/promptugal-news-the-newsroom-we-ran.html)).
- **[The Sovereign Agentic Website](/next/sovereign-website/):** our website cell, grown into its own product at [sovereignwebsite.ai](https://sovereignwebsite.ai/) (opening soon). Built by agents, owned by you.

Neither is a customer's work, and neither is a sale.

## Some agents on a device you hold (a plan)

Some of a cell's agents could run on a Raspberry Pi or an Android tablet. For the sovereign website this is a plan, the appliance, to be offered through a Kickstarter campaign that has not been submitted yet ([launching the appliance](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/launching-the-sovereign-website-appliance.html)). Today cells run in Claude sessions and on ordinary web hosts.
