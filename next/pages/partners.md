# Partners: you bring the business, we build the site

You know people who want a website. We have a team of agents that builds one and hands it over in a GitHub repository the customer owns. You sell it to them, with us building it behind you.

{{figure:team}}

## The sovereignty line

The customer owns the repository; the partner keeps the relationship. Selling a site your customer can take anywhere is not a weakness for a partner: it is the reason to buy from you rather than rent from a platform, and it is why they come back to you for the care around it.

## Three kinds of partner

From Dinis Cruz's [partners sell, we build](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/partners-sell-we-build.html):

- **The freelancer.** One person who talks to people and can say: for this amount, I will give you a website, an agentic email and a workflow. [The freelancer's page](https://agenticcells.ai/partners/freelancer/).
- **The small consultancy.** A firm with customers who want this: a site for each client, the same team every time. [The consultancy's page](https://agenticcells.ai/partners/consultancy/).
- **The larger firm.** Bigger projects, with our orchestration and a network of experts involved, case by case. [The firm's page](https://agenticcells.ai/partners/firm/).

## What you sell: a team, not a template

Even the smallest site is made by seven roles, each played by an AI agent: a conductor, a designer, a developer, a writer, a researcher, a reviewer and a security reviewer, each with a written job description and a behaviour policy, each signing its work. That team is what you resell. [How it works](/next/how-it-works/) says what each one does.

## A deal, from first call to after care

1. **Find.** You find the customer and agree what they want, and the price.
2. **Material.** Their photos, videos and words come in, starting with the interview.
3. **Set up.** We set up the repository, the address and the site.
4. **Designs.** The agents make five designs; the customer chooses, with you.
5. **Hand-over.** The repository moves to the customer's GitHub, within three months at the latest (an expectation we set ourselves, not yet a rule in the terms). You get admin access if the customer agrees.
6. **After.** You host, support, change and charge for it as you choose.

## The terms, in short

The partner terms are published on the live [partners page](https://agenticcells.ai/partners/#partner-terms), and that page is where they are kept. In short, as that page states them: when we are involved, the sale is split 50/50 ([partner terms](https://agenticcells.ai/partners/#partner-terms)); larger projects are agreed case by case; whoever has the customer invoices them and pays the other side its half; payment terms are agreed per deal.

- **Hosting, support, changes and retainers are yours entirely.** We take no part and no share, and we sell no subscriptions.
- **The customer owns** the site, the repository and the material. **You own** the relationship.
- **Our code and policies are open.** You may keep your own repositories and your customers' work private.
- **Nobody is named** publicly, customer or partner, without their agreement.
- **Any country, any language.** You invoice in your own currency, under your own law.

## Your price is yours

Partners set their own price for the build. Our launch price is what we charge; at that price a partner's share of the build is small, so most of a partner's income is meant to come from the care around the site, at prices they set. The terms already allow this.

## A partner who does it alone is still a win

Everything we use is open source. A partner who takes it and builds sites without us is still running the same technology under behaviour policies, and we will say so.

## What is true today

No partner has signed yet and nothing has been sold. The first partners will shape the terms. Dinis Cruz's account of the programme as built is in [you bring the business, we build the site](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/you-bring-the-business.html).
