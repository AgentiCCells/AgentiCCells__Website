## I want a website: the first workflow

This is the shortest route from "I want one" to a person reading your request. It is a proposal of the steps; the end-to-end flow is being built now, the first orders will be run by hand, and nothing has been sold yet. The price is £500, one-off, first proposal, not an offer; Dinis has not yet confirmed a launch price.

1. **Look at the examples.** [The hub](https://hub.agenticcells.site/) has a page for each site we have made, all demos.
2. **Gather what we ask for.** A short description of you or your project, in your own words; photos; and videos if you have them. Some of it can be public already, such as an existing profile; send the link. Nothing else is needed to start.
3. **Email it.** [Open an email to agent@riskmandate.ai](mailto:agent@riskmandate.ai?subject=I%20want%20a%20website%20cell&body=Who%20I%20am%20and%20what%20the%20site%20is%20for%3A%0A%0ALinks%20to%20photos%20and%20videos%20%28or%20I%20will%20attach%20them%29%3A%0A%0AAn%20existing%20profile%20or%20site%2C%20if%20any%3A) with the subject and three prompts already filled in. Attach files or send links. There is no form: the site stores nothing of yours, and neither do we keep your files after handover.
4. **A person replies.** The email goes to a person, who reads it and answers. We have not set a reply time, so none is promised. The reply says whether your material is enough, which price applies, and what happens next.
5. **We build it.** A team of agents builds the site from your material and shows you a preview. You say what to change.
6. **It is handed over.** The site is a static site in a GitHub repository that you own, and you can change it on your own Claude or host it anywhere that serves a GitHub repository. Our part ends there: no subscription and no support from us.

Want a second opinion before you write? The [trust and security page](/trust/) says what our agents can reach and how to stop them. More on the offer is on the [website cell page](/website-cell/).

## Step one: one very simple workflow

Pick one piece of work that repeats. Give it to one Claude agent on a loop, with a written instruction, a place to put its results and a person who checks them. That is the smallest cell. It is a proposal for where to begin, not a product.

If you would rather not set it up, the [website cell](/website-cell/) is the simplest thing we run for you: {{price}}, one-off, first proposal, not an offer. There are no meetings and little of your time. A team of agents builds a public website that you steer from your browser. Nothing has been sold yet, and the end-to-end flow is being built now.

Three very simple workflows to pick from. Each is one agent, one instruction, one place for results and one person who checks:

- **A weekly digest.** The agent reads the sources you name and writes one page of what changed, for you to read and send yourself.
- **A draft inbox.** The agent drafts replies to the same few kinds of message. A person edits and sends every one; the agent never sends. Our [email cell](https://hub.agenticcells.site/cells/cell-email/) is an example, listed on the hub as a demo.
- **A website for you.** The [website cell](/website-cell/) above, and the [job-seeker cell](/job-seeker-cell/), a proposal that does not exist yet.

These are suggestions for where to begin, not products we have sold.

Whatever you run, an agent that sleeps and wakes needs five things: an inbox, timers, memory, permissions and an outbox. [We run all five in our own team](/our-team-as-a-cell/), as files in one repository.

## What it looks like at scale

Our own team ran many agents at Web Summit in Lisbon. The counts below come from one public article, [Ten days at Web Summit](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/ten-days-at-web-summit.md), which counted them from git history and the session list. It says the counts depend on how you count, and that an "agent" means a distinct name in commits.

- Around 250 named agents, across about 20,000 commits in 15 repositories ([the article](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/ten-days-at-web-summit.md)).
- 224 Claude Code sessions, more than 60 of them at once at the peak ([the article](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/ten-days-at-web-summit.md)).

This is our own team's work, not a customer's, and it is a measure of activity, not of quality.
