# The job-seeker cell

A website for someone looking for a job, built by a small agentic team, that shows evidence instead of a list. The person approves every page and sends every message.

- **Status:** a proposal. It comes from a design with mock-ups and invented people. The cell, its templates and the recruiter view do not exist yet. Nothing has been sold.

Source for this page: Dinis Cruz, [Show me the evidence](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/show-me-the-evidence.html), newsroom.sgit.ai, 2026-11-16. Quotes are his words. The mock-ups in it are designs, and its eight job-seekers and five virtual professionals are invented people.

## What it does

Dinis's reason: anyone can now generate a polished CV, so hiring managers trust CVs less. The article cites two surveys. In [Robert Half's survey](https://press.roberthalf.com/2026-03-10-Robert-Half-survey-67-of-HR-leaders-report-AI-generated-applications-are-slowing-hiring) (March 2026), 65% said AI-generated applications make skills harder to verify and 67% said they slow hiring down. In [Greenhouse's survey](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/an-ai-trust-crisis-70-of-hiring-managers-trust-ai-to-make-faster-and-better-hiring-decisions-only-8-of-job-seekers-call-it-fair), 91% of recruiters had spotted candidate deception. We checked the 65% and 91% figures against the two releases on 17 November 2026 ([research/evidence-map.md](https://github.com/AgentiCCells/AgentiCCells__Website/blob/dev/research/evidence-map.md)); the 67% appears in the Robert Half release too.

His answer: "show me the evidence". The cell builds a site of seven pages, each answering a question a hiring manager asks:

1. **Home:** who are you, in one line, with three proofs.
2. **Evidence:** two to four case studies, each with links a reader can open.
3. **Thinking:** how you think and how you use AI.
4. **Me:** the side roads, hobbies and what you are doing now.
5. **A page for you:** made for one employer or one meeting, unlisted and expiring.
6. **Working with me:** a short user manual.
7. **Recruiter pack:** one screen a recruiter can forward.

It also makes the same facts as files that machines read. The site should look like its owner, not like every other AI-built site.

## What you get

- A site of those seven pages, in your own words, with every claim linked to something a reader can open.
- A page made for each serious application or meeting, using public material only, with the employer anonymised if the page is public.
- A recruiter pack.
- A weekly debrief of your search, for you and nobody else (in the design; not built).

## Use cases

Proposals, not a record of customers.

- Someone looking for a job who wants one link to send to employers and recruiters.
- A person preparing for an important meeting who wants a page made for that person.
- A recruiter who wants a shortlist page per role, built from the candidates' own pages, with consent and an expiry date. This is the largest size below.
- A virtual or fractional professional who sells judgement to several clients and wants to show the agent team behind it.

## Behaviour policy

In plain words, as the article proposes it. It is a design, not a measured record.

- Public material only.
- It never invents a fact. A check step compares each fact with its evidence and blocks a page that fails.
- It never sends, posts or applies. The person approves every page and sends every message.
- Employers are anonymised unless they agreed to be named.
