# Creative Commons trademark policy and the "CC" in AgentiCCells (S4)

Checked 2026-11-16 by researcher@AgentiCCells.dinis. Source: https://creativecommons.org/policies/#trademark (page says "Page last updated: 11 Aug 2022"). Read through a page-summary tool, not line by line at source: quotes below should be re-read at the page before anyone relies on them. This is research, not legal advice.

## What the policy says (as read)
- Covered marks include CREATIVE COMMONS (any stylisation or capitalisation), "CC" including the CC-in-a-circle logo and "CC standing alone", CC+, CCPlus, CC0, and the licence buttons and icons.
- Use is authorised subject to the policy, with images taken from the CC website or through software that uses the marks in Unicode.
- Referential use of the corporate logo, and descriptive use of licence buttons and icons (for example to say a work is under a CC licence), needs no permission, but must not imply endorsement.
- Promotional uses (event titles, product names, merchandise) need prior permission from Creative Commons.
- "using the Creative Commons name in a domain name is prohibited without express permission".
- Modified versions of the marks are not authorised (the one exception: a different colour for the CC logo and its background at 3:1 contrast).
- CC may revoke permission if use "confuses the public".
- Contact named for permissions: legal@creativecommons.org (we do not write to it; a person decides).
- Not found: any rule specific to "CC" inside another organisation's name, or a general "confusingly similar" test.

## What this means for AgentiCCells (a reading, not a ruling)
1. Saying the "CC" stands for Creative Commons, and saying our text is under CC BY 4.0 (content/index.md, content/about.md), is descriptive use. Low risk, provided we do not imply CC endorses us.
2. The name "AgentiCCells" puts "CC" (a covered mark, "standing alone") inside a company and product name. That is closer to the "product names" promotional category than to descriptive use, and it is also in the domain agenticcells.ai. The policy does not clearly say a mark embedded inside a longer word is covered, so the risk is unclear, not clearly high. Only Creative Commons can settle it.
3. The logo: do not use the CC-in-a-circle logo, or any modified or look-alike version of it, as part of the AgentiCCells mark. Use of the real CC licence button next to our licence statement is fine if taken from CC's site unmodified.
4. Wording: write "the CC in the name stands for Creative Commons" only with a line saying we are not affiliated with or endorsed by Creative Commons.

## Options for Dinis (a person decides; nothing has been sent)
- A. Keep the name; add a not-affiliated line to the about page and footer; keep the logo free of any CC mark. Cheapest; leaves item 2 unsettled.
- B. As A, and a person writes to legal@creativecommons.org to ask whether the name is acceptable. A draft could go in board/drafts/.
- C. Drop the "CC stands for Creative Commons" reading and let the name stand alone. This removes the link to CC but not the letters.

Researcher's suggestion: A now, B before the name is promoted widely.

## For designer@
Keep the mark text-and-shape only; no circle-C or two-C glyph; no lookalike of the CC logo; no use of CC's logo as ours.
